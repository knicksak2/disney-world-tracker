Pin Frame System — Sample Set

One parametric frame renderer, six rarity tiers, fifteen licensed motifs and two original scene silhouettes. Every pin below comes out of the same code path: a die-cut plate in a tier metal, a recessed enamel well, a raised metal motif, an optional engraved banner, one specular sweep from the upper left, and a cast shadow. Nothing here is hand-lit per pin.

Everything settled so far, everything still open, and what only exists as an assumption. 01 The tier ladder — now six

Six it is. Each step is a materially different substance rather than another shade — bronze, silver and gold are metals, amethyst is a cut stone, pearl is soft moonstone, and the top tier is full spectrum. Black nickel is gone; see the next section for why you were right about it.

02 Tier 6 — you were right, and here's the reason

Your instinct about black nickel was correct, and there's a concrete reason beyond style: the board is dark. A near-black pin on a dark corkboard has almost no edge contrast, so your rarest pin becomes your least visible one. That's backwards. 05 Completion ladder — one castle, six stages

  
On the Mickey ears: there is no safe near-miss. The tri-circle silhouette is Disney's most aggressively protected mark, and "three circles arranged like that" is exactly the thing enforcement is built to catch. But it's worth separating what the ears actually did from the ears themselves — they gave you one instantly recognisable silhouette, repeated at every rung, so the ladder read as a family. Anything can do that job. A castle does it better here, because it's yours and it ties the count chain to the app's hero image.

  
So the ladder is one castle being built: a lone turret, then two, then a wall between them, then gate towers and battlements, then the full castle, then the castle under fireworks. Interior detail — windows, the arched gate — only arrives at stage 5, so the pin gets visibly richer as you climb rather than just larger.

Mapped to achievements from your existing concept, using real CC BY motifs (credited at the bottom). No emoji, no placeholders — this is what would actually ship.

  


  

    Why the motif is metal, not paint. On a real hard-enamel pin the design is raised metal and the colour is poured into the recessed cells between. Filling the motif with the tier metal and the well with enamel reproduces that automatically — and it means every motif inherits its tier for free, so no motif ever needs a bronze version and a gold version.
  


  
  
07 Locked state

  
Locked pins keep the die-cut silhouette in dead pewter with an empty well — you can see the shape of what's missing, which is what actually drives completion. Greying out the finished pin instead would spoil the reveal and read as "already earned."

11 Palette — Royal LOCKED

  
Royal it is. Eight enamel colours, matching the purple and gold the app already uses. Every pin on this page draws from these and nothing else — with one flagged exception: the polished firework ladder (B4) adds six luminous colours plus a spark cream, because all eight of these fail the 3:1 contrast floor against a night sky. The reasoning and the numbers are in the B4 section; it needs your yes or no, since this section is marked locked.

  

  

    One rule the six-tier ladder forces. Amethyst-tier plates are purple, so a purple enamel well behind them turns to mud. Tiers 4–6 (amethyst, opal, black nickel) take their enamel from the dark/cool end only — ink, sky, forest, crimson — never royal or plum. The renderer doesn't enforce this yet; the spec should.
  


  
  
11b Colour separation audit

  
This section exists because you sent screenshots and two things were plainly broken while every test passed: the Medallion's gold motif was invisible on amber enamel, and bronze pins read as gold. Luminance contrast alone misses both — two colours in the same hue family read as one colour at slightly different brightness even at a 2.9:1 ratio. Separation needs hue distance or a real lightness gap, so both are now computed and audited on every pairing the page uses.

  

  

  


  
  
12 The art bible

  
The numbers the renderer already enforces. Locking these in the spec is what stops seventy pins drifting into seventy styles.

  

    
RuleValue

    
Canvas150 × 150 viewBox. Every plate fits within it with a 6px bleed margin for the cast shadow.

    
Motif safe areaCentered circle, 96px diameter on the disc plate, scaled per shape (star 66%, rosette 66%, gem 80%, arch 84%, crest 88%). Motifs never cross the die-cut rim.

    
Minimum feature3px in the 150 space — anything finer vanishes at the 88px grid size.

    
Light directionUpper-left, always. Metal gradient runs 0.15,0 → 0.85,1. One specular sweep, one rim highlight. No exceptions across the set.

    
EnamelMax 3 colours per pin from the one shared palette. Enamel never encodes tier.

    
Three axesMetal = rarity. Shape = category. Motif = the specific achievement. Never crossed.

    
TiersSix: bronze · silver · gold · amethyst · pearl · prismatic. Each is a different substance, not a shade, and luminance climbs monotonically — the rarest pin is the brightest on the board. Tiers 4–6 take enamel from the dark/cool end only.

    
Rarity budgetSeries 1 at 28 pins: 9 bronze · 8 silver · 6 gold · 3 amethyst · 1 pearl · 1 prismatic.

    
Animated tiersPearl (5.5s slow glow) and prismatic (2.4s sweep) only. Motion is a rarity signal, so it must not appear below tier 5. Both honour prefers-reduced-motion.

    
Contained vs die-cutDecided by meaning, not cost. If the achievement has a recognisable object (a castle, a ferris wheel, a turkey leg) it is die-cut. If it doesn't (a rating count, a leaderboard place, a logged total) it takes a contained plate with a motif and a banner. Die-cut rims are a stroke, never an inset copy, so the rim stays uniform on any silhouette; the fill rule flips to nonzero so interior cells fill with enamel and pick up metal frames.

    
Silhouette sourcingPreferred: cut an existing library icon — no new geometry, so none of the hand-authoring defects are possible. Fallback: draw it in a vector editor and export the path. Not sanctioned: hand-computing coordinates (that is how the stage-2 height bug, the inconsistent spire overhang and the stale dieTop values all got in) or composing from the rectangle/triangle primitives (only makes fortress shapes).

    
Die-cut silhouette testA silhouette is only usable if it is compact, closed and chunky. Three disqualifiers: wide-and-thin, dependent on internal negative space (which vanishes when the interior fills with enamel), or no distinctive outline. Failing this test sends the achievement to a contained plate — having an object is not enough, the object needs a readable silhouette.

    
Cost of a silhouetteMeasured in defect risk, not time. Every hand-authored path needs its invariants asserted: on the y=480 baseline, centred on x=256, declared top matching its actual topmost point, and monotonic height across a stage sequence. That cost is paid once in test code and amortises across the set.

    
Die-cut rim by tierbronze 4.2px · silver 5.2 · gold 6.2 · amethyst 7.2 · pearl 8.2 · prismatic 9.2. Rarer pins show more metal, which both fixes the tier-legibility gap and makes rarity physical. Rim is specified in screen px and divided by the die scale, so it never varies with silhouette size.

    
BannerOptional engraved metal ribbon, max 4 characters, centred at the shape's bannerY. Motif auto-scales to 88% and shifts up when present. Never on the star plate — no room.

    
Motif anchorcenter (default) or ground. Ground sits the motif baseline on the well's lower inner edge, for scene pins.

    
Render sizes44 (chip) · 88 (grid) · 138 (detail) · 300 (unlock) · 200-in-512 (share card).

    
LockedDead-pewter plate, empty well, motif at 10% white, padlock badge. Silhouette stays readable.

  


  

    One open technical decision. react-native-svg has patchy filter support, so the cast shadow and inner shadow may not survive on device exactly as they render here in a browser. Two options: drop the filters and carry the depth with gradients only (slightly flatter, stays vector and scales free), or add a build step that pre-renders each pin to PNG at @1x/@2x/@3x plus the 512 share size with the filters baked in. I'd pre-render — the pins are the one place where the polish is the product.
