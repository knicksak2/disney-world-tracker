Proposal 01 · First-Run Flow

Intent-Based 3-Step Onboarding (Learn by Doing)

Runs once right after RegisterScreen (skippable, re-openable from Settings). Instead of a boring tooltip tour, it asks why they're here, lets them complete one rewarding action in 20 seconds, and gives a 4-line map of the tabs.

Interactive · Try all 4 intents
9:41 5G 100%

Why this works better than a coach-mark tour

  • Routes to the right mode immediately: Disney guests are either planning a trip, in the parks today, or logging past memories. Asking upfront lets Step 2 and the landing screen match their real goal.
  • Instant "aha" moment in Step 2: If they pick Tracking history and tap 3 rides they've been on, they immediately see a Bronze Pin unlock preview and populate their stats — teaching the core loop (Log → Stats + Pins) without a single paragraph of instructions.
  • Always skippable, zero trap: Top-right Skip drops them straight onto Home with the dismissible Start Here card still available.

What each Step 1 choice triggers in Step 2

  • 🗓️ Planning an upcoming trip: Minimal 2-field Quick Trip Starter (name + optional dates) — skips lodging, guests, and budgets until they're inside the trip.
  • 🎢 In the parks right now: Pick today's park (with live wait averages) → lands directly on that park's Live Waits & Today view.
  • ⭐ Tracking past rides & food: 12-ride "Tap what you've ridden" starter grid → bulk-logs completions & unlocks their first pin.
  • ✨ Just looking around: Same quick-pick grid (optional) or jumps straight to the 4-tab map.

Persistence & Re-entry

Store a tiny onboardingCompletedAt + initialIntent flag (user profile or local storage). Add a "App Tour & Guide" row in Settings so anyone can replay Step 3's tab map anytime.

Proposal 03 · Feature Discoverability

Universal "Jump to Anything" Search in HomeSearchBar

When an app has 50 features, even returning users forget whether Crowd Calendar is in Explore or Trips, or where the Dining Plan Calculator lives. Indexing app tools right inside search gives everyone an instant escape hatch.

Interactive · Type or tap a keyword
9:41 5G 100%
Search Anything
🔍

How it works technically (zero backend cost)

  • Keep a tiny static client-side registry (~12 entries) of App Tool Shortcuts with keywords & synonyms (e.g., ["dining plan", "meal plan", "worth it", "calculator"] → Dining Plan Calculator).
  • When the user types in HomeSearchBar, filter the local tool registry synchronously (0 ms latency) and render up to 3 matching App Tools above the live /catalog?q= experience results.
  • Each result shows a clear breadcrumb (e.g., Trips › Dining Plan) so the search result also teaches the user where that tool lives for next time.
  • When the search input is empty and focused, show Popular Tools chips (Live Waits, Crowd Calendar, Dining Plan, Hidden Mickeys) so new users discover hidden gems passively.

Edge case: Trip-scoped tools when user has 0 trips

If someone searches "budget" or "dining plan" before creating a trip, tapping the shortcut can open a tiny prompt: "Budget & Dining Plan live inside a Trip — create a quick trip to start?" (or for Dining Plan Calculator, open the standalone calculator directly if supported).

Phase-to-Tools Mapping (all 11 existing routes preserved)

  • 📅 Early Planning (>60 days out): Booking Timeline (60-day dining alert!), Planned List, Trip Budget, Dining Plan Calculator.
  • 🎒 Final Prep (1–60 days out): Schedule Builder & Optimizer, Reservations, Packing List, Planned List.
  • 🎢 Active (In the parks today): Today's Schedule, Reservations, Trip Activity Feed, Expenses (log on the go).
  • 📸 Past Trip: Trip Summary, Trip Album, Expenses & Settle Up, Trip Activity.

Why power users lose zero speed

Nothing is hidden behind a mode toggle. Every one of the 11 hub sections (HUB_SECTION_DEFS) remains accessible directly below under 4 scannable categories: Itinerary, Money & Dining, Prep & Stays, and Crew & Memories. First-timers only need to look at the top 4 cards; power users tap straight into any group.

Note on existing tests

TripDetailScreen.tsx has a tripStructureRegression test pinning HUB_SECTIONS. Keeping the canonical 11 definitions intact and organizing their presentation by phase + group keeps the data contract clean while dramatically lowering visual density.
