Disney World Tracker — IA Redesign v2 (Kiro's proposal)

Two changes from the first draft: the tab bar itself now reconfigures between planning and in-park use instead of staying fixed, and the old Profile "junk drawer" contents are relocated to where each thing is actually used — not just repackaged into a tidier version of the same drawer.

Global chrome — a header notification bell and, in in-park mode, a header ⋯ More — now carry things that don't deserve a permanent tab slot but still need to be one tap away from anywhere.

CHANGED FROM V1 1. The tab bar is no longer static. It reconfigures based on the same active-trip signal ActiveTripShortcut already computes — no manual toggle, no user setting.
2. Notifications moved out of Profile entirely into a global header bell, reachable from every screen.
3. Stats, Pins, and Food History/Lists moved out of "Me" into Explore → My Progress — they're about your history with the catalog, not your identity, so they now live next to the catalog.
4. "Me" shrank to three things: identity, friends, settings. No segments needed anymore — it's short enough to be one flat screen.
5. In-park mode promotes Waits and Log to their own tabs (the two highest-frequency in-park actions); Explore and Me are demoted to the header ⋯ More sheet rather than removed.
0 The tab bar reconfigures — it doesn't just toggle content underneath a fixed bar
My first draft kept one fixed 4-tab bar and only changed what rendered inside "Today." That's a half-measure: while walking around a park, "Explore" (browse the catalog + crowd calendar) is dead weight, and the two things people actually reach for constantly — live waits and quick logging — were buried a tap deeper inside Today instead of being tabs themselves. 3 Global chrome — reachable from anywhere, not tab-bound
Two things don't belong on any tab at all: notifications (a cross-cutting inbox — invites, rode-with tags, pin awards, shares) and, only while in-park mode, the two demoted destinations.
🔔 Notification bell — lives in every header, in both modes. Today it's a button buried at the bottom of Profile's scroll; here it's global chrome with a live badge count, same pattern as the Inbox/Sent unread count. Tapping it opens the existing NotificationCenterScreen as a sheet — no navigation stack push, no tab dependency.

Where things ended up, and why they're not still under Profile

FeatureOld homeNew homeWhy
Stats Profile → "View your stats" button Explore → My Progress Coverage/ratings stats are a record of your history with the catalog. That's a property of the catalog, not of your identity — it belongs next to Browse and the Crowd Calendar, not next to your avatar.
Pins Profile → "View your pins" / "My Showcase" Explore → My Progress (gallery) + a claim banner surfaced in Log while in-park Pins are earned by doing things in the catalog, so the full board lives with progress. But the moment-of-claiming needs to happen where you are when it fires — in Log, not three taps into a settings-shaped screen.
Food History / Lists Profile → two separate buttons Explore → My Progress Restaurants are catalog experiences; your food history is a filtered view of the same "what I've done in the parks" data as ride completion. Grouping it with Pins/Stats instead of scattering three progress features across the app.
Notification Center Profile → "View notifications" button Global header bell, every screen, both modes It's cross-cutting by nature (trip invites, rode-with tags, pin awards, shares) — tucking it inside one tab's scroll is the opposite of how time-sensitive alerts should be reachable.
Friends / Shares Its own top-level tab (5th slot) Me Friend comparison and sharing are real but lower-frequency than Home/Explore/Trips. Folding it into Me — rather than spending a tab slot on it — is what frees up the room that used to force everything else into Profile.
Push prefs, password, log out Profile, bottom of the scroll Me → Settings (still there — this is genuinely account settings, its natural home) Not everything needed to move. Account security and preferences are correctly modeled as identity-adjacent; only the misfiled "progress" and "alerts" content needed to leave.
Live Waits A card inside Home, one tap into a detail view Its own tab, in-park mode only ("Waits") Checked constantly while walking around — arguably the single highest-frequency action in the app during a visit. Promoted to a tab specifically because planning-mode Home/Explore don't need it at all.
Quick logging (ride/snack/pin claim) A 4-button dock crammed into Home Its own tab, in-park mode only ("Log") Same reasoning as Waits — high frequency only in one context. A dedicated tab gives it room to grow (e.g. "Share Moment") without fighting for space against the next-up card and the party feed.
Scope note. Still a static visual mockup — no navigation code, screens, or routes were changed. It reuses the app's real theme tokens (#5b2a86 primary, #f6c343 accent, park hues) and existing screen/component names (ActiveTripShortcut, CrowdCalendarScreen, PinBoardScreen, StatsStack, NotificationCenterScreen, FriendsListScreen, InboxScreen) so the mapping to real components stays unambiguous. The biggest open question this raises, worth flagging honestly: a tab bar that changes shape when a trip goes active is a bigger engineering lift than one static bar (two tab configs to maintain, and a transition moment where the bar visibly reshuffles under the user's thumb) — and it's a navigation pattern with real precedent (Google Maps swaps its whole bottom sheet/nav when driving starts) but not a common one in this app's category. Worth testing on a real device before committing, not just judging from a static mockup.
