# App name research: Magic & Main, Magic Explorer, and new candidates

Read-only research. No repo source was touched. Not legal advice. No `.env` files were read.
Label key: **[V]** verified by a tool call in this session. **[I]** inferred.
Dates in search snippets are as the search tool reported them.

## 1. Summary answers

- **Magic & Main: No-go.** Disney-fan businesses already use all three phrasings, and each is a hard conflict on its own.
  - "Main & Magic" is a Disney parks podcast with a site (mainandmagic.com), a Patreon, an Etsy shop and a Facebook community.
  - "Magic on Main Travel" is a Disney-specialist travel agency that writes its name with a registered-trademark symbol.
  - "Magic on Main Street" is a Disney fan site.
  - Disney-travel agencies also use "Main Street Magic". A planning app called Magic & Main would be read as one more of these.
- **Magic Explorer: Caution, lean away.** No exact App Store match [V]. It is not unique enough: the "Magic ___" prefix is crowded in this niche, and magicexplorer.com is parked for sale.
- **Best new idea: Gate & Main.** It keeps the "X & Main" rhythm they liked. It has no App Store hits on exact or contains [V], and no web brand found [V, weak]. gateandmain.com and .app show no DNS records [V].
- **Ranked top 3 across everything:** 1) Gate & Main, 2) Main Attraction (already screened clean), 3) Footlights.

### 2c. Disney-association risk [I]

- "Main Street, U.S.A." is Disney's land name. "Magic" plus "Main" reads to a fan as "Magic on Main Street".
- Disney is not the more likely complainant. "Main" alone is not Disney's mark, and nothing here uses Magic Kingdom or Main Street U.S.A. as a whole.
- The nearer risk is a small Disney travel agency or the podcast sending a notice. They sit in the same field (Disney trip planning), under near-identical names, and have a registration (®) or established use.
- Apple App Review guideline 5.2 (intellectual property) covers third-party marks. A complaint from any of these owners could trigger a review hold or takedown. The exposure comes from their prior use, not from Disney.
- Fans may also assume it is an official or affiliated app. That raises the odds of a complaint.

### 2e. Fit [I]

- Label: "Magic & Main" is 12 characters. iOS truncates at about 11 to 12, so it is borderline. "Magic and Main" (14) will truncate.
- The ampersand is allowed in App Store names (e.g. "Cork & Journey", "Love & Magic®: Spellfyre" are live apps) [V]. The domain, URL slug and bundle ID must spell it "and" or drop it. Users will mistype "Magic and Main" and "Magic & Main" both, so search is split.
- It does not describe the app: no waits, crowd, pins or expenses. It leans only on a Main Street nod.
- It is memorable and warm, but that is exactly why others use it.

- Domain: magicexplorer.com resolves with NS afternic.com, so it is parked for sale [V]. magicexplorer.app has no records [V].

  - MagicWait for Disney World (App Store).
  - MyMagicTrip: WDW Planner (App Store, GB).

## 5. Ranked top 3 (across everything, including previously cleared names)

1. **Gate & Main**: closest in feel to Magic & Main, no "Magic" crowd, no trademarks, domains free by DNS.
2. **Main Attraction** (previously screened clean; user said it "isn't bad"): the safest word-play on Main Street, and it describes the star feature.
3. **Footlights**: clean on the App Store, evokes Front Row in showbiz language. Costs a domain workaround.

## 6. What I could not verify

- USPTO status: I did not run a TESS/Trademark Search. The ® on Magic on Main Travel is unverified as a registration.
- App Store Connect name availability: the iTunes API only shows live apps, not reserved names.
- Google Play: only checked by web search, not directly.
- DNS only suggests registration. "none" does not prove a domain is free.
- Web search does not support exact-phrase matching, so "no brand found" means "not surfaced in the top 10", not "does not exist".

## 7. Next steps

1. Search the USPTO at tmsearch.uspto.gov, classes 9 (software), 41 (entertainment), 42 (SaaS) and 39 (travel planning), for the top 3. Include phonetic variants (Gate and Main, Gait & Main).
2. Search Google Play directly for each top name.
3. Check availability at a registrar (not just DNS) for the .com and .app of the chosen name, and check social handles.
4. Pick one primary name and one backup. Reserve the App Store name in App Store Connect early (it holds the name).
5. Keep "Disney" out of the name and subtitle. Describe the app as "a trip planner for Walt Disney World" only in the description, and review Apple guideline 5.2 wording before submission.
