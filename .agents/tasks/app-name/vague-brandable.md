# Park-agnostic, vague, brandable names: 531 generated, 40 ranked
Read-only. No repo source or .env file was touched. Scratch scripts and CSVs are in `%TEMP%\naming-vb`. Not legal advice.
"No brand found" means "not surfaced in top results". The web search tool is not exact-phrase, so it is weak evidence. Scores are 1-5 for Sound (S), Fit (F, works for many parks), Warmth (W) and Clearance (C). C is tied to recorded evidence (5 = nothing anywhere; 4 = no same-name brand; 3 = adjacent or crowded same-lead-word brands; 2 or less = blocked). Label = visible characters of the name. "&" names use the `and` form for domains (e.g. `pocketandpip.com`); the `pocketpip.com` form was not checked.

## 1. Summary answer
- **No name here clearly beats the existing four.** The best new names, **Pocket & Pip** and **Glee & Go** (17), only tie Keepsake Days (17) and sit above Penny & Pin (16), Merrywander (15) and Magic Explorer (14) by one or two points on my own scores. Treat that as a tie, not a win.
- **Pocket & Pip is the closest match to the user's taste.** It is a companion name like Park Pal. Pip is a small friendly thing, and "pocket" is the phone. Both domains are unregistered (RDAP and DNS agree) and the name has no App Store match in five query forms. Cautions: the reversed name "Pip & Pocket" is a Photo & Video app, and "&" needs a slug.
- **The invented-word family mostly fails on clearance.** Of 449 new candidates (82 more were already in earlier reports and were skipped), 207 had an exact App Store hit (F3). The surviving coinages (Hellie, Twirlie, Gathri, Frolica) have `.com` registered, are near other brands, or say little. The "X & Y" and "X Pal/Pip/Lark" families survived best, and most of them have both domains free.
- **Several attractive names were blocked** by brands in travel: Wanderlark (travel agency + app), Wanderpal (trip-planner), Lantern & Larks (UK glamping), Skip & Hop (Skip Hop family gear), Wanderjoy (itinerary app, kills the Joy + Wander combos). See section 5.

## 4. Honest top 10 and how it compares
1. **Pocket & Pip** (17). Companion feeling, both domains free, clean in all five query forms. Weakness: "&" and the reversed Photo & Video app.
2. **Glee & Go** (17). Strongest feeling and rhythm, both domains free. Weakness: "Glee" TV show and travel-adjacent Glee apps.
3. **Honey & Hop** (16). Easy to say, both domains free. Weakness: "Hop" is a crowded travel syllable.
4. **Joylark** (16). Single clean word, no travel brand found. `.com` taken.
5. **Wonderpip** (16). Pip + wonder, no brand found. `.com` taken.
6. **Little Lark** (16). Warmest for the small-companion taste. "Lark" has travel brands, so C is only 3.
7. **Marigold Pal** (15). Warm and soft. Marigold is used by other companies, none in travel that I found.
8. **Hello & Hop** (15).
9. **Yonder Day** (15). "I planned it on Yonder Day" reads naturally; Yonder/Yondry planner apps nearby.
10. **Hello Wander** (15). Best explicit fit; the Wander word is crowded.
**Do any beat the earlier four?**
- **Keepsake Days (17), Penny & Pin (16), Merrywander (15), Magic Explorer (14):** Pocket & Pip and Glee & Go tie Keepsake Days on my numbers and Pocket & Pip is warmer. They do not beat it on evidence. I would not switch from Keepsake Days or Merrywander on this report alone.
- **Where these new names are better:** the "Pip/Pal" companion shape matches Park Pal, which the user liked. Keepsake Days and Merrywander lack that shape.
- **Where they are worse:** "&" names need a slug and read as shops; no new name carries a Disney-fan wink like Magic Explorer. If the user wants a friend-like name, test Pocket & Pip next to Merrywander.

## 7. What I could not verify
- **USPTO and state trademarks:** not searched. "Glee" (a Fox TV mark), "Pip", "Penny" and "Marigold" are common marks in other classes; classes 9, 41, 42 and 39 need a real search.
- **Google Play directly:** only through web search results.
- **Registrar status:** RDAP 404 means unregistered today, not that the name can be bought at standard price. For "&" names only the `...and...` domain was checked.
- **Web search depth:** about 30 targeted searches (Wanderlark, Pocket Penny, Lantern & Lark, Little Lark, Wanderpal, Joylark, Skip & Hop, Honey & Hop, Pocket & Pip, Penny & Pip, Wondie, Gatherie, Lantern Pal, Marigold Pal, Glee & Go, Hello & Hop, Tilly & Tuck, Pin & Penny, Hellie, Joyette, Frolica, Wimsy, Dusk & Daisy, Peach & Pin, Wonderpip, Wanderpip, Joypip, Moonpip, Gleelark, Sunday Hop/Yonder Day combined). Rows marked "not individually searched" had App Store and domain checks only. Instagram, TikTok, Etsy and podcasts are poorly covered.
- **Languages:** checked from my own knowledge only, not by a native speaker. No awkward meaning found for the top 15, except: "Penny" is a European grocery chain (Penny & Pip, Pin & Penny); "Gaily" has a modern double meaning; "Hop" means hops in Dutch/German; Hellie is near German "hell" (bright), harmless.
- **API under-reporting:** the iTunes API missed real apps in earlier runs, so a zero is not proof.
- **Scores** are my judgment; only Clearance is tied to the recorded evidence.

## 8. Recommendation
1. For the Apple Developer enrollment, use a name that is already cleared: Keepsake Days or Merrywander remain safe, and the name can change before release.
2. If the user wants a companion name, shortlist **Pocket & Pip** and **Glee & Go** and run a USPTO search on both.
3. Do not spend more time on invented one-word coinages; this run found none with a free `.com` and a good Fit score.
4. Nothing was implemented.
