## 0. Summary answers
- **Parksfolio: not blocked by your exact-name filter, but it is ParkFolio plus one letter.** Same-category app, same sound, same logo-level look. Dispute odds **low-to-medium**, downside is a forced rename. The Parkeon/Flowbird US marks are dead [V, unofficial]. It is the cheapest of the risky options, not a clean one.
- **Mousetrack: smaller than I made it sound, still a literal F1 hit.** The Disney tracker at mousetrack.co.uk is a 2022 hobby-scale UK site (79 X followers, GBP only, no company, no app). Legal exposure from it in the US is low [I]. The real costs are practical: `.com` is held by someone else, Google would show them first, and a UK storefront launch would meet them head-on.
- **Track family: workable as plumbing, weak as branding.** "Track" is descriptive, so only the prefix is ownable and every sibling needs its own clearance. A competitor, **Magic Track: Theme Park Log**, already uses "<word> Track" for Disney World logging (press kit lists release 2026-11-16, today) [V].
- **Best `<flavor>track` names:** Rope Track (16/20), Churro Track (15), Lanyard Track (15), Streetlamp Track (14), Firework Track (14). None beats Moseybug-class names on clearance, and **new evidence lowers Moseybug**: 12 App Store apps start with "Mosey", including a Travel trip planner (section 4).

**Domains (RDAP)** [V]: `parksfolio.com`, `parksfolio.app`, `parksfolio.net` all 404 (unregistered). `parkfolio.com` (Parkeon, registered 2000) serves only a country picker with "Copyright 2009 - Parkeon".
**Trademarks (unofficial, Trademarkia search page)** [V]: `parksfolio` 0 results, `parkfolios` 0, `park folio` only generic FOLIO marks. `parkfolio` 2 results, both Parkeon and both dead: PARKFOLIO (serial 76126230, filed 2000-09-11, classes 9 and 38, cancelled 2014-05-02) and PARKFOLIO NEO (serial 76624003, filed 2004-12-08, cancelled 2016-10-14). Parkeon's old Madrid/EU/other-country marks were not checked.
**What a real search needs:** the new USPTO Trademark Search with `parkfolio`, `parksfolio` and wildcards `park*folio`, in classes 9, 39, 41, 42; then TSDR for any hit. Also check EUIPO/WIPO for Flowbird/Parkeon.

**Confusability** [I]: one letter apart, 7 vs 8 letters. Spoken, "park-FOH-lee-oh" and "parks-FOH-lee-oh" are hard to tell apart. US practice generally treats a plural of a mark as the same mark [I, general rule, not checked here]. Goods overlap: both are phone apps that log park visits with ratings and spend. Both sit in Travel. Differences: National Park Service passport stamps vs Disney trip planning.
**Dispute likelihood: low-to-medium.**
- *Parkeon/Flowbird:* low. US marks dead, different field (pay-and-display terminals).
- *ParkFolio developer:* low-to-medium. Individual developer, no registered mark found, one release, 0 ratings. But they hold earlier use (March 2026) and the right to complain to Apple at any time. The risk rises only if your app gets visible.
- *Apple name check:* I could not test it. Parksfolio is a different string, so I expect App Store Connect to accept it [I].
**Downside:** forced rename after launch (new name, icon text, store listing, domain, and any shared "Track"-style account branding). Code cost is small if the name stays in config; brand cost is the real loss.
**Verdict:** if you want the folio idea, Parksfolio is usable at accepted risk. Foliobug and Merryfolio from `parkfolio.md` avoid the issue entirely.

## 2. Mousetrack, deeper (Task B)
**What the site is** [V]: `mousetrack.co.uk` is a React single-page app (Create React App bundle, Cloudflare Pages analytics, Google Analytics). Title "Mousetrack.co.uk: Find Disney World holiday prices, deals and discounts". It tracks Disney World hotel, flight and ticket prices for UK holidaymakers: prices in GBP, UK departure airports, a TUI logo. Footer: "(c) Mouse Track 2023 - contact@mousetrack.co.uk". Disclaimer: "in no way affiliated with The Walt Disney Company". `/about`, `/privacy`, `/terms` return the same app shell (no static text), `robots.txt` is open, `sitemap.xml` is the shell. No affiliate network, ads, company name or number, address or app-store link in the bundle. PWA manifest only.
**Age and size** [V]: `mousetrack.co.uk` and `.net` registered **2022-06-16** (Nominet and Verisign RDAP), renewed to 2027-06-16. X account @MousetrackNet joined **July 2022**: 79 followers, 641 following. Wayback shows the Disney tracker from 2022-07-05 onward (title "Mousetrack"), snapshots through 2026-09-13.
**Earlier life of the domain** [V]: Wayback snapshots from 2018-08 to 2020-10 show "MouseTrack | Cloud based GPS tracking and more for device, car & fleet users" (UK WordPress site). So the name was a UK GPS brand before 2022; whether it still trades under another domain is unknown.
**Other names** [V]: `mousetrack.com` registered 2004-12-01, expires 2027-12-01, redirecting (302) since 2019, 403 to bots; owner unknown. `mousetrack.app` unregistered. Companies House: no company named Mousetrack (search "mousetrack" returned none; "mouse track" returned unrelated "Mouse" companies). So the operator is probably an individual [I].
**US presence:** none found. No US storefront, USD prices, US trademark or app.
**US trademark (unofficial Trademarkia)** [V]: `mousetrack`, `mousetracks`, `mousetracker` 0 results. Two-word `MOUSE TRACKS` has marks: a 2023 pet-toy application (abandoned 2025) and a 1983 greeting-card mark (cancelled 1991). The list was long and truncated, so I cannot say no Disney-owned MOUSE TRACKS mark exists. UKIPO not checked.

**Risk read**
- *Legal:* trade-name rights are territorial. A free UK site with 79 followers has, at most, UK passing-off rights [I]. A US app is unlikely to draw a US claim from it [I]. The exposure appears if you publish on the UK App Store storefront, or if the owner files a UK or US mark first. You can exclude the UK storefront in App Store Connect.
- *Practical (the larger cost):* search results for "mousetrack disney" go to them. `.com` is owned by a third party. A Disney World app and a Disney World price tracker share one audience, so reviewers and forums will confuse them. The plural "Mouse Tracks" is the Disney Channel show title (earlier report).
- *What would change the picture:* the owner files a mark or adds an app; they pivot to US content; you add a price-tracking feature; you launch in the UK; or Universal expansion makes "Mouse" the wrong word anyway (see section 3).
- *Net:* acceptable if you knowingly take a name that Google will split with someone else. It stays a literal F1 hit, so I would not call it clean.

## 3. The Track family (Task C)
### 3.1 Evaluation as a naming system
- **What it implies:** one umbrella ("Track account") plus one app per park family, named `<flavor>track`. Naming-wise, "Track" becomes the shared surname and the flavor word is the only distinctive part. Sign-in sharing is backend work and independent of the names [I].
- **Descriptor problem:** "track" is the most common word in this category. Of 184 flavors I screened, 47 (26%) already have an exact App Store name ending in Track, Tracks or Tracker (PinTrack, CrewTracks, SnackTrack, Ride Track x2, Gatetrack, PennyTracker). Existing theme-park trackers: LogRide, Parky!, Parkventure, ParkBound, Parkonaut, RideTracker, Attractions: Ride Tracker, Track Record: Ride Log [V]. Only the prefix is protectable [I].
- **Direct neighbour:** Magic Track: Theme Park Log (Magic Digital LLC, Mount Dora FL; Travel; $2.99/mo; "Magic Track for Disney World" in its own screenshots) [V]. A family named `<flavor>track` reads as a copy of its format, and "Magic Track for Disney World" suggests that format could extend to other parks [I].
- **Shared suffix helps recall a little, hurts search:** users who learn "X Track" will type "track" and meet Magic Track, LogRide and the rest first [I].
- **Crews spanning both park families:** with separate listings, a trip with two parks needs two apps, two purchases (Apple pricing and reviews are per app) and a cross-app trip/crew/expense store. A single app with park-family modes avoids that and keeps a vague name. The family only works if trips, crews and spend live server-side under the account [I].
- **Each sibling needs its own clearance:** with this suffix every new app means a new App Store, domain, web and trademark pass. The umbrella "Track account" is not ownable as a bare word [I].
- **If you keep it:** treat "Track" as a subtitle ("<Name>: Disney Track") and pick distinctive stems, or choose a less crowded family suffix (3.4).
