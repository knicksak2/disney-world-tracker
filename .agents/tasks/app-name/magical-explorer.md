# Name clearance: "Magical Explorer"

Read-only research. No repo source was touched; no .env files were read. Not legal advice.
Note: sources and App Store dates in this session's results run into late 2026, so "now" below means that window.

## Verdict: NO-GO (do not use as the public name)

1. **Exact-name collision with a live, mature-rated franchise.** "Magical Explorer" (stylised Magical★Explorer) is a Japanese light novel and manga licensed in English by Yen Press (manga Vol. 1 is rated "18+ M (Mature)"). A TV anime started airing 2026-10-03, and it is on Crunchyroll and Disney+ Japan. The story is about an eroge (adult dating game). Searching the name returns anime pages, not your app, and the first-page results are adult-themed.
2. **Weak Disney link, and it is the wrong kind.** "Magical" evokes Disney marketing but does not point to Disney World. It reads as generic fantasy or kids' game. Several Disney marks use the word (below), so it adds trademark risk without adding a recognisable connection.
3. **The "Magic ___" slot in the Disney-planner niche is already occupied.** "Magic Guide" (registered ®) has many Disney apps, and MagicMapper, MagicPin, MagicWait and MagicTable also exist.
4. **Poor label and ASO fit.** 16 characters will truncate on the iOS home screen (about 11-12 visible). "Explorer" is a high-competition word (file managers, puzzle and kids games).
5. The App Store itself is clear (no exact-name app), so this is a search-clutter and reputation problem more than a store-block problem.

## Evidence by check

### 1. Apple App Store (US), iTunes Search API (verified, run via PowerShell)
Terms run: Magical Explorer, MagicalExplorer, Magic Explorer, The Magical Explorer, Magical Explorers, Magical Explorer Disney. Each returned 31-47 results.
- **Exact-name match for any variant: none found.**
- Names containing both words: none. Closest "Explorer" apps: Explorer Quest (TRAKID, Lifestyle, 1,107 ratings), Jigsaw Puzzles Explorer (Doodle Mobile, 54k), Jigsaw Explorer, Urbex and Monument Explorer, TerraScope: GPS Map & Explorer, Country Explorer.
- Closest "Magical" names, all games: The Magical Typewriter, Magical Creatures, Magical Maintenance Mystery, Magical Hands. Results are mostly fantasy and kids' games.
- "Magical Explorer Disney" returned the real niche: My Disney Experience (official, 3.16M ratings), Magic Guide for Disney World (VersaEdge, 16.9k), Wait Times for Disney World (VersaEdge), Disney World Lines (TP), MagicPin: Disney Pin Collector, MagicWait for Disney World, MagicTable: Mouse Watcher (5.8k), Mouse Life: Disney Checklist, WDW Magazine, Orlando Theme Park Day.
- Web result: **MagicMapper for Disney World** (apps.apple.com id6744375166) does route tracking, ride check-ins and food logging. It overlaps your features and sits next to "Magic ___" names.
- Caveat: the API is a keyword search. Absence of an exact match does not cover apps unpublished, region-limited or removed.

### 3. Disney-association risk (inferred, not legal advice)
- Disney marks using these words: Magic Kingdom, MagicBand, My Magic+, Disney Magical World (3DS game), Disney Magic Kingdoms (game), "Most Magical Place on Earth". Magic Guide® and "Magic" are used by third parties, so "magic" is not Disney-exclusive.
- "Magical Explorer" is probably not read as official Disney by itself. Combined with Disney screenshots, castle art or the words "Disney World" in the title, a rights holder or an App Review reviewer could object (Apple guideline 5.2). Use "Disney" only descriptively in metadata, with a clear "unofficial" note, and keep the name free of Disney terms.
- The bigger practical risk is the anime franchise's rights holders in the same name, and association with its adult content.

### 4. "Explorer" collisions (inferred)
- Windows File Explorer and Internet Explorer (Microsoft), Ford Explorer, Explorer scouting programs (Boy Scouts of America Exploring/Explorers). Different classes, so low trademark risk, but they crowd search results.
- Disney also has "Explorers" themed products. I did not verify specific marks. Check them in the USPTO search.

### 6. Fit assessment (judgement)
- Describes the app? Partly. "Explorer" suggests exploring parks, but nothing says trip planning, crew, reminders, wait times or the pin vault.
- Tone: generic and kid/fantasy-game sounding. Your app is a utility for adult planners with a collection side.
- Label: 16 characters, truncates. Memorability: low, since it sounds like many games.
- ASO: "Explorer" and "Magical" are both crowded keywords. The anime dominates web search for the exact phrase.

## Recommendations
1. Drop "Magical Explorer" and its variants.
2. If you want to keep the explorer idea, candidates I screened on the US App Store (and one web search):
   - **Park Pathfinder**: App Store has no app by that name (only an unrelated "PathfinderPAL"); web search showed nothing for the exact phrase. 13 characters, still long for a label. Looks clear, not guaranteed.
   - Rejected after screening: Park Scout (taken: "Park Scout: Disneyland Guide", "ParkScout Magic"), Park Compass (taken: Theme Park Compass: WDW Routes, themeparkcompass.com), Park Explorer (many "Parks Explorer" apps, crowded), Parkfinder (several "ParkFinder" apps).
3. Before any public name: manual USPTO search in classes 9 (software), 41 (entertainment) and 42 (SaaS) at tmsearch.uspto.gov; direct Google Play search; register the domain and handle; then have a trademark attorney clear it for a public launch.
4. Keep the Disney connection in the subtitle and keywords ("for Walt Disney World"), as a descriptive use, rather than in the name.
